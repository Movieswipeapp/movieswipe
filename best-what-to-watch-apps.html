The best "what to watch" apps for iPhone in 2026, compared

These apps get lumped together, but they solve four different problems: deciding with someone, deciding alone, finding out where something streams, and tracking what you've seen. Picking the wrong category is why people install three of these and keep none. Here's what each is actually for.

Disclosure: MovieSwipe is our app. We've tried to be straight about where the others beat us, because a comparison that wins on every row isn't a comparison. Prices and features were checked in August 2026 — verify current pricing in the App Store before subscribing to anything.

MovieSwipe — if the problem is deciding, not finding

Good at: ending the "what should we watch" conversation in under a minute. Shared sessions let two or more people swipe the same deck separately and surface only the films everyone said yes to. The plain-language search is the differentiator — you can ask for "something like Whiplash but less stressful" instead of stacking genre filters, and it handles requests filters can't express. Native on Apple Vision Pro, which almost nothing in this category is.

Not good at: telling you which service a specific film is on, tracking what you've already watched, or reviews and ratings from a community. If you want a permanent record of your viewing life, this is the wrong app.

Price: free to download; Pro is €2.99/week or €6.99/month. iOS 18.2+ and visionOS 2.2+. No Android version.

Matched — the best-known couples app

Good at: exactly one thing, done simply. Two people swipe, matches appear. It has been around longer than most of the alternatives, has picked up mainstream press coverage in the US, and it exists on both iOS and Android — which matters a lot if you and your partner are on different platforms. If that's your situation, Matched is probably the right answer and MovieSwipe isn't an option at all.

Not good at: steering what you see. There's no plain-language search, so you're largely working with the deck you're given.

Movies + Friends — for groups larger than two

Good at: movie nights with several people, where the hard part is finding a film four people will all accept rather than two.

Not good at: everything outside that use case. It's a single-purpose tool.

Reelgood — the most complete streaming guide

Good at: being the front end for every service you subscribe to. You tell it what you pay for, and it shows you one unified catalogue with accurate availability, plus tracking and a genuinely good browse experience. If your problem is "I have six subscriptions and no idea what's on them", Reelgood beats everything else here, MovieSwipe included.

Not good at: deciding with another person. It's a browsing tool, and browsing is the thing that takes twenty-five minutes.

JustWatch — the availability lookup

Good at: answering "where can I watch this specific film, and what does it cost." It covers a very large number of countries and services, it's free, and it's fast. Keep it installed regardless of what else you use.

Not good at: helping you decide. It answers questions about films you've already chosen.

Letterboxd — for people who love films, not just watch them

Good at: logging, rating, reviewing, and following people whose taste you trust. The lists other users make are one of the better discovery mechanisms that exists.

Not good at: Tuesday night at nine o'clock with someone who doesn't care about film criticism.

Trakt — the tracking backend

Good at: automatically recording everything you watch across devices and services, with deep statistics and an API. Serious tracking, mainly for people who want the data.

Not good at: being the app you open when you don't know what to watch.

Which one should you pick?

You watch with a partner and the deciding is the problem. Get a swipe app. MovieSwipe if you're both on iPhone and you want to steer the deck with language rather than filters; Matched if one of you is on Android, because MovieSwipe is iOS-only.

You have several subscriptions and can't see across them. Reelgood. Nothing else here does that job as well.

You just want to know where a film is streaming. JustWatch, and it's free.

You care about films as a hobby. Letterboxd, plus one of the above for the practical part.

Most people end up with two: one that decides and one that finds. Those are different jobs and no single app currently does both well.
