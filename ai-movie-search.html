Find films by describing them, not filtering them

Genre filters can express "comedy, 2010s, over 7.0". They cannot express "something funny that won't make me think about my job". MovieSwipe's search takes the second kind of request. Below are real prompts and the sort of thing they return.

Why filters run out

Most of what you actually want from a film isn't a metadata field. Tone, pace, how heavy the ending is, whether it demands your full attention, whether it's safe to watch with your parents in the room — none of that is a genre. So you approximate: you pick "comedy", get four hundred results, and start scrolling again, which is the problem you were trying to avoid.

A language search skips the approximation. You describe the film you want the way you'd describe it to a friend, and the deck comes back shaped like that.

Twenty prompts that work

By comparison to a film you already like

"Something like Whiplash but less stressful" — obsessive-craft dramas with lower tension: Sound of Metal, The Peanut Butter Falcon, Chef, Ratatouille, Hoop Dreams.

"Like Arrival but without the sad ending" — thoughtful sci-fi that lands somewhere warmer: Contact, The Martian, Interstellar, Star Trek: First Contact.

"A comedy like Palm Springs but shorter" — high-concept comedies under 100 minutes: Game Night, Groundhog Day, Booksmart, The Nice Guys.

"Knives Out but not a whodunit" — ensemble films with the same playful, well-made feel: The Grand Budapest Hotel, Ocean's Eleven, Clue, The Nice Guys.

"Something with the vibe of Before Sunrise" — two people, one conversation, one city: Lost in Translation, In Search of a Midnight Kiss, Weekend, Past Lives.

By mood and situation

"Something funny that isn't stupid" · "A film I can fall asleep to without missing anything" · "Something tense but not violent" · "A feel-good film that isn't sentimental" · "Something to watch when you're hungover" · "A film that will make me cry, on purpose" · "Beautiful to look at, don't care about the plot"

By constraint

"Under 100 minutes, nothing depressing" · "Something my mum and I can both watch" · "A film for a group of four with completely different taste" · "Nothing with subtitles, I'm doing the dishes" · "Something set somewhere cold" · "A good film from a country I've never seen a film from" · "Something from before I was born that still holds up" · "No romance, no wedding, no airport scene"

How to write a prompt that works

  • Name a film you liked, then say what to change. "Like X but shorter / lighter / stranger" is the single most reliable pattern.
  • Say what you don't want. Negatives narrow faster than positives — "no jump scares", "nothing about illness", "not three hours".
  • Describe the evening, not the genre. "It's late and we're both tired" is more useful information than "drama".
  • Ask for one thing at a time. Five constraints at once tend to return the intersection of nothing.
