Boilerplate — 100 words

MovieSwipe is an iOS and Apple Vision Pro app that turns picking a film into a sequence of yes-or-no choices instead of a scroll through a catalogue. Users swipe right on films they want to see and left on the ones they don't. In a shared session, two or more people swipe at the same time on their own phones, and the app surfaces only the films everyone said yes to — removing the negotiation that makes movie night take half an hour. A plain-language search lets users describe what they're in the mood for instead of stacking genre filters. MovieSwipe was released in 2025 by independent developer Casper Steenhoven and is available in eight languages.

The angle, if you need one

Streaming solved the supply problem and created a decision problem. People routinely spend twenty to thirty minutes browsing before they watch anything, and a share of those evenings end with nothing watched at all. MovieSwipe borrows the swipe interface from dating apps for a specific reason: judging one option at a time stays cheap no matter how long you do it, while comparing hundreds of options gets more expensive the longer you look. The shared session adds a second effect — because neither person sees the other's rejections, nobody has to turn down a partner's suggestion out loud.

What MovieSwipe is not

We'd rather be described accurately than generously. MovieSwipe is not a streaming-availability guide — it won't reliably tell you which service a film is on. It is not a watch-history tracker or a review community. It is iOS-only, with no Android or web version. It is a small independent app with a small review count.
